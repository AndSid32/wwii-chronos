When Was D-Day? Normandy, June 6, 1944

The short answer: D-Day was June 6, 1944, when Allied forces landed in German-occupied Normandy, France. It was the opening day of the Normandy invasion.

What happened?

American, British, Canadian and other Allied forces landed on five beaches: Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno and Sword. Airborne troops also landed inland. Nearly 160,000 Allied troops landed in Normandy on the first day.

Why was D-Day important?

The landings established an Allied foothold in Western Europe. They did not end the war in one day: the Normandy campaign continued through the summer, while the Soviet Union fought Germany on the Eastern Front.

Key dates

June 6, 1944Allied landings in Normandy
June 27, 1944Allies capture Cherbourg
August 1944Allied breakout and liberation of Paris

“D-Day” is a general military term, but in popular usage it usually refers to June 6, 1944.
