Why Did Japan Attack Pearl Harbor?

The short answer: Japan attacked Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, hoping to weaken the United States Pacific Fleet and secure time for further expansion in Southeast Asia.

What happened?

Japan’s war in China and expansion in Asia brought increasing conflict with the United States and other powers. U.S. economic restrictions, including an oil embargo, put pressure on Japanese leaders. Japanese planners saw the U.S. Pacific Fleet as an obstacle to their intended offensives farther south.

Did the attack achieve Japan’s aim?

The strike killed more than 2,400 Americans and damaged ships and aircraft. But it did not prevent the United States from entering the war: the U.S. declared war on Japan on December 8. Germany and Italy declared war on the United States on December 11.

Key dates

December 7, 1941Attack on Pearl Harbor
December 8, 1941United States declares war on Japan

The attack was part of a broader Japanese strategy in Asia and the Pacific, not an isolated event.
