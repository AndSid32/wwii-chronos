Harvard Mark I Presented to Harvard University

1944-08-07 · KEY EVENT

IBM formally presents one of the early automatic digital calculators to the university.

On 7 August 1944, IBM president Thomas Watson Sr formally presented Harvard University with the Automatic Sequence Controlled Calculator, known as the Harvard Mark I. The machine was the result of a long development process associated with Howard Aiken's ideas.

The large electromechanical calculator used relay and mechanical components and could execute sequences of operations automatically. Its physical size illustrates how different early computers were from modern electronic devices.

Although vacuum-tube electronics soon surpassed electromechanical systems in speed, the Mark I played an important role in the development of automated computation and in training a new generation of specialists.

The date 7 August marks the machine's formal presentation and transfer, not the first conception or the start of funding. Both stages are important to understanding the long development of computing technology.
