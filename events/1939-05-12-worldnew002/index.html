First Funding for the Harvard Mark I

1939-05-12 · ARCHIVE EVENT

The automatic electromechanical calculator project received its initial funding.

On 12 May 1939, the first appropriation was granted for the future Harvard Mark I computing machine. The project was initiated by Harvard researcher Howard Aiken, who wanted to automate lengthy mathematical calculations.

Development was carried out with IBM and drew on experience with electromechanical machines, relays and punched cards. The project required a large number of components and careful synchronisation of mechanical and electrical assemblies.

The machine was not completed in 1939: the Harvard Mark I was formally presented to the university in August 1944. This entry therefore concerns the start of funding, not the computer's entry into service.

The story of the Mark I shows how investment in automated calculation before the war contributed to the development of computing. Such machines later became part of the broader transition from mechanical calculators to programmable systems.
